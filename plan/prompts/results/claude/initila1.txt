type: explanation
In Japanese, greetings change depending on the time of day.
おはよう (Ohayō) is used in the morning — think of it as 'good morning'.
こんにちは (Konnichiwa) is used during the day, and こんばんは (Konbanwa) in the evening.
Japanese is written in three scripts: hiragana, katakana, and kanji. We'll start with hiragana.

type: explanation
はい (hai) means 'yes' and いいえ (iie) means 'no'.
Japanese sentences often end with か (ka) to turn them into a question — similar to a question mark.
The particle は (wa) marks the topic of a sentence. For example: わたしは (watashi wa) means 'as for me' or simply 'I'.

type: explanation
Numbers 1–10 in Japanese: 一(ichi), 二(ni), 三(san), 四(shi/yon), 五(go), 六(roku), 七(shichi/nana), 八(hachi), 九(ku/kyū), 十(jū).
Japanese counts change depending on what you are counting — people, animals, and flat objects all use different counters.
For now, just focus on learning the base numbers 1–10.
